# Disruption of skin homeostasis reveals dynamic remodeling of the human skin-resident memory T cell pool

Tissue-resident memory T cells (TDRM) are critical regulators of tissue immunity, but how their diversity is remodeled during human immune reconstitution remains poorly understood. Here, we longitudinally study human skin TDRM following allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) using single-cell RNA and T cell receptor sequencing, complemented by flow cytometry, spatial imaging, and host-donor chimerism analyses. HSCT induced transient remodeling of the skin TDRM compartment, characterized by reduced type 17 (T17)-associated features and relative enrichment of T1-associated states. This remodeling occurred independently of clinically manifest graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) and was accentuated in GVHD. Donor-derived T cells acquired tissue-resident features but showed limited representation of T17-associated states, while both host- and donor-derived cells contributed to the altered TDRM composition. Importantly, cells with T17-associated phenotypes recovered during long-term immune reconstitution, supporting dynamic and reversible remodeling of the skin TDRM population
